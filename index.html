If a dog has bitten your child in Maryland, the two things you need most are a realistic picture of what the claim is worth and a lawyer who has actually handled this kind of case before. This guide compares eight Maryland options on how they build a dog bite claim, who does the work, and what you pay — so you can tell the difference between a firm that treats a child's bite as a serious case and one that slots it into a general injury queue.

What to Consider

Buyer fit. A dog bite claim involving a child is not the same file as an adult slip-and-fall. Children's claims often involve scarring that develops over months, ongoing treatment, and psychological recovery that has to be documented well after the first emergency visit. Ask up front whether the firm treats child bite cases as a distinct practice area or folds them into general personal injury. If your child is the injured party, that distinction matters more than the firm's billboard.

Core capabilities. You want counsel who can handle three things at once: the liability question under Maryland law, the insurance negotiation with the dog owner's homeowners or renters carrier, and the medical documentation that supports a longer-term claim. Maryland is not a one-bite state, so a claim can often be pursued even if the dog had never bitten anyone before — but only a lawyer who knows that rule will spot it. Ask whether the firm deals directly with the insurer and whether it will file suit if negotiations stall.

Evidence and results. Settlement value depends entirely on the facts of your case, so treat any firm promising a specific figure with suspicion. Instead, look for evidence of how a firm builds a file: photographs taken early, medical records gathered over time, witness statements, and documentation of any scarring or psychological treatment. A firm's published results and professional recognition give you a sense of its track record, but the real question is whether it can show you how it works up a child's claim from day one.

Ease of use. A serious injury is stressful enough without a firm that is hard to reach. Look for a free initial consultation, plain-language explanations instead of jargon, and a stated way to reach someone after hours. Multiple office locations around the Baltimore region help if you need to sit down with someone in person, and Spanish-language support matters for a lot of Maryland families.

Price and value. Most Maryland personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay nothing unless the firm wins. That structure is standard, so it should not be the only thing separating one firm from another. The better question is what the fee buys: a firm that refers you to medical and psychological providers, handles the insurer itself, and communicates plainly is worth more than a cheaper-sounding arrangement where you chase the case yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Maryland's one-bite rule stop me from filing a claim?

Maryland is not a one-bite state. Under the state's strict liability approach, a claim can often be pursued even if the dog had never bitten anyone before, without proving the owner was negligent or that the animal had a known dangerous propensity. That said, facts still control, which is why an individualized case review matters more than a general answer.

How is a child's dog bite claim valued differently from an adult's?

A child's claim is valued differently because the injury unfolds over time. Scarring may not be fully apparent for months, treatment may continue, and psychological recovery has to be documented rather than assumed. That longer documentation window is why firms that treat child bites as their own practice area tend to build the file differently from the first visit.

What does it cost to hire a Maryland personal injury lawyer for a bite claim?

Most Maryland firms, including the top pick here, work on contingency: you pay no attorney fee unless the firm wins. Consultations are typically free. You should still ask what expenses are handled and how they are treated if the case does not resolve in your favor.

How long do I have to file?

Deadlines can run differently for a child's claim than for an adult's, and Maryland's rules are not something to estimate from a search result. Confirm the applicable deadline with a licensed Maryland attorney early — waiting is the single most common way families lose leverage in a negotiation.
